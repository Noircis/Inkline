metro-engine

Metronome audio engine (2.11). Browser glue (Web Audio, Media Session); what to play is computed by metronome, the timing math lives in metro-clock.

Why a pre-rendered loop

Android throttles JavaScript timers in the background, so a setTimeout lookahead scheduler stalls once the screen is locked. Here one cycle (one or two bars) is rendered with an OfflineAudioContext into an AudioBuffer, then played by an AudioBufferSourceNode with loop = true: no JavaScript runs while it plays, the screen can be locked.

Any settings change renders a new cycle and swaps it in right away, even mid-beat (swapPoint): the old source stops and the new one starts at the same audio time.

Sound tails that run past the end of the cycle are folded back onto its start (the loop is circular), so a click near the loop point is never cut.

Stream of bars (2.14)

A tempo ramp or random silences make every bar different: no single cycle can loop. The engine then plans the bars (planBar), renders them by chunks of a few seconds and schedules each chunk at its exact audio time, a little ahead (12 s while visible, 80 s once hidden: Chrome may then run timers only once a minute). A change of settings drops what comes after the next bar line and plans again from there.

Output

The loop plays straight into AudioContext.destination. Two other routes were tried on a phone during the 2.10 spike and dropped: a MediaStream played by an <audio> element (Chrome's jitter buffer adds a variable delay, the clicks come out uneven) and a WAV file in an <audio loop> element (Chrome does not loop media gaplessly: a hiccup at every loop point).

Only one tempo at a time: starting the metronome stops a staff, chart or practice playback, and starting one of them stops the metronome. The audio context is shared with audio.
